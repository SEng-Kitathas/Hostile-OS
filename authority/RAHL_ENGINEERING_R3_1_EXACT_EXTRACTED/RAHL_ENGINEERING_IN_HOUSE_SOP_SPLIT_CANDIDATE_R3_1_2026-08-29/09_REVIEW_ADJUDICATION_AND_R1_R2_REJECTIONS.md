# Review Adjudication and R1/R2 Rejections

Two external review modes converged on the same center: authority preservation and verifier binding. The informed review also challenged the unmeasured churn premise; the blind review exposed cold-start loss that project memory could otherwise hide.

## R1

Rejected as a replacement candidate. It flattened authority classes, omitted active cold-start detail, and its verifier missed most of its own declared rejection conditions.

## R2

Also rejected as a replacement candidate. It fixed several structural checks, but all 36 rules still carried one peer candidate authority label. A fresh hostile pass also showed four resealed mutations that survived: inverted K06 body, mandatory K18 body, registry-statement mutation, and deletion of all related-rule groups.

R3 therefore returns to exact source classes/bodies instead of trying to infer equivalence from a shorter peer kernel.

New scars:

- `SCAR_RECORDED != SCAR_EMBODIED`
- `DECLARED_REJECTION_CRITERION != IMPLEMENTED_REJECTION_CHECK`
- `SAME_SURFACE != SAME_AUTHORITY`
- `FALLBACK_AUTHORITY != COLD_START_RECOVERABILITY`
- `REGISTRY_HEADING_BOUND != RULE_SEMANTICS_BOUND`
- `ARCHITECTURAL_HYGIENE != CHURN_HYPOTHESIS_CONFIRMED`

## R3.1 narrow correction

R3.1 changes verifier authorship and binding precision only. It removes an unused import, adds function type contracts, and replaces global prose-substring acceptance with exact parsed human-section comparison. The frozen machine binding remains the primary integrity anchor; human text parsing checks that the reviewed live section is the section that carries the expected meaning.
